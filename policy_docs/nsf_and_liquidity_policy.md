# NSF (Non-Sufficient Funds) and Liquidity Stress Policy

## Isolated vs. Chronic NSF Events
- Zero NSF events in the trailing 3 months: no adjustment.
- One NSF event, with no repeat occurrence in the same statement period:
  treat as an isolated incident. This should be named as a minor risk
  factor but should not, by itself, move an applicant out of the Low or
  Moderate band if all other signals are healthy.
- Two or more NSF events in the trailing 3 months, or NSF events clustered
  within the same billing cycle: treat as a liquidity-stress pattern rather
  than an isolated incident. This is a material risk factor and typically
  warrants Moderate-to-High scrutiny even when bureau score and DTI are
  otherwise acceptable.
- NSF events combined with new short-term financing activity (BNPL,
  payday-style advances) opened in the same period: treat as a compounding
  signal of active cash-flow strain, not two independent minor factors.

## Distinguishing Timing Issues from Insolvency
An NSF event followed by a normal deposit cycle and no further incidents
is more consistent with a timing mismatch (e.g., a bill posting before a
paycheck cleared) than with insolvency. Reflect this distinction in the
financial story narrative when the bank statement data supports it -- do
not treat every NSF event as equally severe without this context.
